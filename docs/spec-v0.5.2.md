# Mutu: Spec v0.5.2 (Standalone)

**v0.5.2 change (Oct 26, 2026, decided from the delegation-sweep model, `simulation/results-split-49-28-23.md`):** the payout split is now **49% producer / 28% work / 23% sovereign dividend**. The 2% commons share comes off the top first, unchanged. This replaces the v0.4-v0.5.1 split. The producer remains the largest single slice. Work and dividend together now take the 51% majority. Nothing else changed. Schedule, cap, epochs, tail, commons, and fees are untouched.

**v0.5.1 change (Oct 22, 2026, approved on the smoothing model, `simulation/smooth.py`):** the hump's steps are **smoothed**. Rewards now run 8 → 12 → 18 → **26 → 26** → 18 → 12 → 8 → 4 → 2 → tail 1.0. The old schedule ran 6.25 → 12.5 → 25 → 50 → 25 → 12.5 → 6.25 → tail. No step anywhere is worse than Bitcoin's 2:1 halving. The old schedule ended in a 6.25× cliff into the tail. The intergenerational ratio falls 8.0× → 3.25×. Epoch 0's budget rises 28%. Total hump issuance is 28.14M over 40 years. Founder's call on further smoothing: with the tail fixed at 1.0, 2:1 is the finest feasible step quantum. The schedule stops here.

**v0.5 changes (Oct 22, 2026, approved on design modelling):** (1) The per-system cap is now a **watt cap. 80W per system.** It is implemented as a frozen credited-TH/s schedule (§5). It replaces the doubling TH/s cap. The variant model (`simulation/results-variants.md`, `results-audit.md`) showed doubling disenfranchised entry devices by epoch 3. Dividend lost. Gini → 0.81. Cap-filling "systems" at 4,272W. The watt cap keeps every design ratio constant in every epoch. The scored sweep (40-200W) picked 80W. 100W was a close second. (2) **Epochs compressed to ~4 years** (210,000 blocks). The hump completes in 28 years. It peaks at years 12-16. It totals **28.875M** coins before the tail (HUMP-28. Identical annual issuance to the old 56-year shape in every epoch). Everything else carries from v0.4.2 unchanged.

*The coin's name is **Mutu** (adopted Oct 22, 2026). Mutualism in two syllables. "Infralist" survives as the design's internal codename. Preliminary conflict screening found no existing coin named Mutu. The nearest neighbours are Mutuum Finance (ticker MUTM, an unrelated DeFi lending token) and a dormant MUT token. A screening result, not a legal clearance.*

*Oct 22, 2026. The security-and-payouts revision. v0.4 keeps every principle the founder locked. Basement mining. Bitaxe-class citizen hardware. Per-system cap. No industrial ASICs. Infrastructural mutualism. v0.4 rebuilds two things the earlier drafts got wrong or left soft: **who earns the new supply** (§7) and **how hard the network is to kill or capture** (§4, §8). Modelled in `simulation/model_v04.py`. Results in `simulation/results-v0.4.md`.*

*What changed vs v0.3.1: (1) payouts are now **49% producer / 28% work / 23% sovereign dividend** in every epoch. The block producer earns the largest single slice of the new supply. Work and dividend together take the 51% majority. Everyone who works receives something. The dividend goes only to sovereign (non-delegated) systems, weighted by √work, so smaller systems earn a higher dividend rate per unit of work. (2) Delegated/pool work is legal but **dividend-ineligible**. Pool producer payouts must be distributed to contributors by protocol rule, with the operator's fee capped. (3) Identity hardening. Anonymous multi-vendor attestation. An **activation queue** that makes a flash majority take ~8 windows minimum. Producer/dividend eligibility only after two full windows of credited work. (4) Hard window finality is **removed**. Chain selection is most-accumulated-work like Bitcoin. Rewards settle only after a full window, so reorganizations cannot claw back paid income. (5) The commons can fund software but can **never change consensus rules**. Issuance, caps, eligibility, and attestation roots are frozen at genesis.*

*Status labels: **FOUNDER'S RULE** = a founder decision. **FIX** = mechanism added in v0.3 to make a rule workable. **DEFAULT** = a design default number, changeable by the founder. **RESIDUAL** = risk that remains after the fix, stated not hidden.*

## 1. The founder's locked decisions (accumulated)

1. **Infrastructural mutualism is the goal** (Swartz 2018). The coin exists to revive **basement mining**. Small operators at home, like the 2017 home-rig precedent.
2. **Per-node computing-power cap**, rising at each halving/epoch to track technology. *(v0.5 refinement, approved: the cap is denominated in watts. 80W/system. It rises in credited-TH/s terms via a frozen efficiency schedule. §5.)*
3. **No pooling as subordination.** Originally: one node cannot work under another node's template, key, or payout. Refined in v0.4 (§7): pools may coordinate, but delegated work is dividend-ineligible and pool payouts are protocol-enforced. A pool can organize miners. A pool can never own their rewards.
4. **Long epochs**, not Bitcoin's ~4. *(Set at ~8 years in v0.1-v0.4. v0.5 sets epochs to ~4 years / 210,000 blocks on the variant model. Approved Oct 22, 2026.)*
5. **Issuance starts small and grows** toward a peak. Kept as the *hump + tail* (§5). Rewards are shared between miners in every epoch via the §7 work and dividend slices. v0.4 supersedes the original "sharing starts at a certain epoch" phasing.
6. **Industrial ASICs (S19 class and up) do not participate.** Not throttled. Not two-tier. Out.
7. **Laptops/general computers do not mine.** Mining programs on a personal machine are intrusive (founder experience). Citizen hardware is **any Bitaxe-like dedicated small miner**. Open class. Any maker or DIY build. Small. Quiet. Cheap.
8. **Development path.** The founder does not code. Spec → simulation → testnet → audited launch with outside devs.
9. **The block producer earns the largest single slice of the new supply** (decided Oct 22; share revised to 49% Oct 26, 2026). Every participating miner receives coins. The miner whose system produces the block takes the largest slice of that block's issuance. Work and dividend together take the 51% majority.
10. **Pools may operate, but a pool earns no dividend** (decided Oct 22). Pooled/delegated work can win producer rewards more often in aggregate. The mutualist dividend belongs to individual systems. **Smaller miners receive it at a higher rate** than larger ones.
11. **The chain is fully autonomous, and the founder exits at launch** (decided Oct 22). No admin keys. No operator. No founder privileges. No premine. The founder launches the network. The founder's own small miner is **system #1**. First to register. Ordinary in every other respect. Same bond, same queue, same cap, same rules as system #10,000.

## 3. The node system: the unit of the whole design

**DEFINITION (FIX).** A **node system** is:
- **One node controller** running the full node software (validates, stores the chain, builds its own block templates). Either integrated in the same enclosure (FutureBit Apollo precedent: full node + miner in one desktop box) or a Pi-class companion on the same home network, under the same owner.
- **Hash units** (Bitaxe-class boards) attached to that controller, in any number.
- **One identity key**, held in a secure element (§4).
- **One bond** (§6).

Rules that follow from the definition:
- The **cap applies to the node system's total credited work**, not per board. Five boards past the cap earn exactly what one board at the cap earns: the cap. Stacking boards buys redundancy, not income. *(The 2017 home-rig precedent, formalized: many GPUs, one rig, one miner.)*
- A **factory**, if one exists, is many node *systems*. Each with its own controller, identity, and bond. Co-location was always allowed (founder's rule). What it can no longer do is amortize one identity across a warehouse of hash.
- **Class envelope for hash units (FOUNDER'S RULE, DEFAULT numbers):** dedicated miner, ≤200W per unit, home-quiet (≤~40 dB), open schematics/firmware, low-hundreds-of-dollars class. Laptops, desktops, GPUs, and industrial ASICs are not hash units. By the identity rule in §4, not by inspection.

## 4. Identity: the secure element is the wall, and also the meter

**The problem this solves.** With SHA-256 as the puzzle, an S19's work is cryptographically valid. Exclusion therefore cannot come from the puzzle. It comes from **whose work the chain will credit at all**:

1. **Only registered node systems may mine.** A block or share not signed by a registered identity key is invalid, regardless of proof-of-work attached.
2. **The identity key lives in a secure element** (e.g., an ATECC608-class chip, ~$1) designed into the open device spec. The key is generated inside the element and cannot be exported.
3. **No gatekeeper (FIX for gap 4).** Secure elements ship from their silicon vendors with **factory certificate chains** proving the element is genuine. Registration = presenting that chain + the element's public key on-chain, plus starting the bond (§6). Trust roots are the silicon vendors (several are accepted, not one). *Not* the founder, not a foundation, not a manufacturer whitelist. Anyone who buys a genuine secure element and builds or buys a class device can register. Including DIY builders, which keeps the 2017 DIY culture inside the design.
4. **The element is a rate meter (FIX for gap 5).** Shares count only if **co-signed by the secure element**. The element enforces a maximum co-signing rate in hardware/firmware at the epoch cap. An S19 feeding work through one attested identity therefore gains nothing. The meter will not sign faster than a class device could work. The identity's credited work is capped. The non-outsourceable puzzle construction (Miller et al. 2015) means the S19 doing the actual guessing could steal the reward anyway. So the arrangement is both useless and unsafe for the operator.
5. **Legacy path (FIX for gap 12).** Devices without an element (today's Bitaxes) may register a *software* identity and mine **pro-rata only**. The equal dividend (§7) requires an attested identity. Sharing starts at epoch 3 (~year 24). The installed base has a full hardware generation to turn over. No early supporter is stranded at genesis.
6. **Rotation (FIX for gap 13; MANDATORY: decided Oct 22, 2026).** At each epoch boundary every identity rotates to a fresh key. Bond carried over automatically. Longitudinal linkability of a device's history breaks. Mandatory, not optional. Privacy that depends on each operator remembering to opt in is privacy most systems never get.
7. **Anonymous attestation (v0.4 hardening).** Registration uses group/anonymous attestation (DAA-style). A system proves "genuine metered element from an accepted vendor + this exact identity has not registered before" via a one-time nullifier. Without revealing the element's serial number. Miners are not publicly trackable by device. Vendors cannot enumerate who mines. One element physically cannot mint two identities. A duplicate nullifier is a cryptographic fraud proof.
8. **Activation queue (v0.4, FIX for gap 16).** Registration is open to anyone. A new identity becomes eligible to *produce blocks and receive the dividend* only after (a) its bond is filled, (b) it has two full windows (56 days) of credited work, and (c) it clears the activation queue. Activations per window are capped at **10% of the eligible set at that window's start**. New systems earn the work slice (§7) from day one (escrowed until activation). Joining is never pointless. A flash majority is mathematically impossible. Doubling the eligible set takes ≥8 windows (~224 days) even if one buyer takes every activation slot, in public, while honest systems keep activating too. **Genesis bootstrap:** the queue engages once the eligible set reaches 1,000 systems. During the seed phase (which includes the founder's system #1) activation requires only the bond + two windows of work. The network can be born before it can be defended by the queue. Seed-phase systems may *produce* blocks from registration. The chain cannot wait 56 days for its first block. All producer, work, and dividend earnings are **escrowed until activation completes**. Difficulty starts at minimum and adjusts normally. A single small rig can carry the chain alone at the start, exactly as the first Bitcoin nodes did.
9. **Money keys are not mining keys (v0.4.2).** The secure element holds only the *identity* key. It can sign work and attestation. It can never spend a coin. Every system registers a separate **payout address** controlled by the operator's own wallet key, held wherever the operator chooses (the controller's wallet by default; a hardware wallet or multisig is recommended and needs no permission). All producer, work, and dividend payouts go to that address. Consequences: a stolen, hacked, or dead miner loses its mining identity. Never its earnings. A lost wallet key loses the coins but not the identity. No mining device is ever a theft target for the coins it earned. Non-mining users simply hold ordinary wallet keys, exactly as with Bitcoin.
10. **Hardware death (v0.4.2 clarification).** The element lives with the **controller**, not the hash board. Hash units are hot-swappable (§3). A dead board is replaced in minutes. The system keeps its identity, bond, and activation, losing only the downtime's work. If the **element itself** dies, that identity is gone with it (the key was non-exportable by design). The operator registers a *new* identity on new hardware, earns the work slice from day one (escrowed), and re-activates on the standard path. Bond refilled from the new system's earnings, or instantly from an existing balance, since the payout wallet is untouched. Plus two windows of credited work. The dead identity's un-exited bond is stranded (5 coins; the price of making fake-death bond cycling pointless). No earnings are ever lost to hardware death.
11. **Compromised-root containment (v0.4, FIX for gap 17).** Accepted vendor roots (minimum three independent vendors) are **frozen into the genesis block**. No online certificate checks. No revocations. No root updates except a voluntary hard fork. If objective fraud proofs (duplicate nullifiers / double-registration) from one root exceed a protocol threshold, **new registrations from that root pause automatically**. Existing identities from every root, including that one, keep mining and the chain never stops. One vendor's failure degrades onboarding for that vendor. Nothing else.

**RESIDUAL.** A secure element can be physically attacked by a sufficiently funded lab. A corrupt element supply chain is a trust assumption. Cost per fake identity stays ≥ (genuine element + device + bond + time). That is the actual security claim. Not perfection. v0.4 shrinks what a failure can reach. It does not repeal the assumption.

A two-epoch peak plateau. A glide down in steps never worse than 2:1. No era's miners wake up to a cliff. The two halves of one lifetime. Now a 40-year lifetime.
- The **tail** (Monero-precedented permanent emission) removes the peak cliff. It funds the sharing dividend (§7) forever at a small, shrinking inflation rate. A maintenance wage for the commons, not a scarcity asset. This coin is not digital gold. It does not claim a hard cap.

A frozen table, never governable):

Because the cap is defined in watts, a system's maximum power, heat, and noise are bounded **forever**. Hashrate per system floats up with efficiency, equally for everyone. The modelled income ratios hold in every epoch (entry ≈ 30% of a maxed system, Gini ≈ 0.28). See simulation/results-audit.md.

## 6. The bond: self-funding (FIX for gap 6)

- **DEFAULT: 5 coins per node system.** Deliberately small. The device itself (~$60-400) is the real Sybil price. The bond is friction, not a stake. It must never grow into proof-of-stake by the back door.
- **Nobody needs coins to start.** On registration, the protocol **escrows the node's first earnings** until the 5-coin bond is filled. At epoch-0 scale with 10,000 systems, average earnings ~41 coins/system/yr. Bond filled in ~6 weeks. Faster for early/smaller networks. Until the bond is filled the node earns the work slice (§7) normally but is **not producer- or dividend-eligible**. §4's activation queue and two-window work history also apply. Dividend farmers and flash attackers alike must be long-lived identities by construction.
- Bond is returned on clean exit. Slashed only for *provable* fraud (e.g., the same element co-signing work for two identities).

## 7. Payouts: producer earns the largest slice, everyone who works earns, small sovereigns earn the dividend (v0.4; split revised v0.5.2)

Every block subsidy, after the 2% commons share (§9) is taken off the top, splits the same way in **every epoch**. The phased 50/75/90 dividend of v0.3 is retired. It paid the block producer least, which is backwards.

| Slice | Share | Who receives it |
|---|---|---|
| **Producer** | **49%** | The system whose valid block is accepted: plus **all transaction fees** in that block |
| **Work** | **28%** | Every system with credited work in the window, pro-rata to capped credited work, paid directly |
| **Sovereign dividend** | **23%** | Qualifying **sovereign** systems only, weighted by **√(credited work)** |

- **Producer slice (FOUNDER'S RULE).** Producing the block is the scarce, useful act. It earns the largest single slice of the new supply. 49% of the subsidy plus all fees. A system's chance of producing is proportional to its credited work, which is capped per system (§3). Bigger home systems win more often. Nobody wins by industrial scale.
- **Work slice.** Participation itself pays. Any registered system that submits credited work in a window receives this slice pro-rata. Including systems whose work is delegated to a pool (below). Nobody who actually works earns zero.
- **Sovereign dividend (FOUNDER'S RULE, √-weighted).** Qualification (all required): attested identity (§4), bond filled (§6), activated (§4 queue + two windows of history), work was **not delegated** in that window, and sustained credited work ≥ **25% of the average registered system's credited work** that window (the v0.3.1 relative threshold. Self-scaling, never absolute). The slice is split by **√(credited work)**. A small system earns a *higher dividend per unit of work* than a large one, while a large system still receives a larger total dividend. Modelled (epoch 3): a Gamma-class system's dividend rate per TH/s is ~2.3× an at-cap system's. Fallback: if the qualifying set is ever empty, the slice rolls into the work pool. Issuance is conserved in every window, as in v0.3.1.
- **Pools (FOUNDER'S RULE: legal, bounded, dividend-free).** A pool may coordinate any number of systems. Observable delegation is what the protocol prices. Work submitted under a pool's template/payout key is **delegated work**. Delegated systems still receive the work slice directly. When delegated work produces a block, the producer slice goes into a **protocol-locked pool payout** that may only be spent pro-rata to the contributing systems' credited work in that window, with the **operator's fee capped at 5%**. A pool cannot custody, redirect, or quietly keep its members' producer rewards. Delegated work earns **no sovereign dividend** for that window. A system returns to sovereign status the window after it resumes building its own templates. Effect, modelled: at 20% delegation, a delegated Gamma earns ~59% of an identical sovereign Gamma's expected income, with much lower variance (CV 0.06 vs 0.28). Pooling remains a real choice (stability for income), never the dominant strategy. A pool identity itself never receives a dividend.
- **Settlement.** Window accounts settle **one full window after the work window closes**. Producer rewards mature on the same schedule. Rewards are only ever paid from blocks deep in the most-work chain (§8). A reorganization can change *unpaid* calculations but can never claw back settled income.
- **Worked scale (epoch 3, 10,000 systems, model, after the commons share, at 20% delegation):** sovereign Gamma ≈ 63 coins/yr expected (63-140 across 20-80% delegation). Sovereign Apollo-class ≈ 330 (330-529). Delegated Gamma ≈ 37 with near-zero variance (flat across delegation shares). Per-system income still falls as the network grows. The promise is access, a producer's premium for useful work, and a dividend that tilts toward the small. Not a wage guarantee.

## 8. Security model: built to be very hard to kill or capture (v0.4)

This coin's security is **not** pure proof-of-work. v0.4 does not pretend otherwise. It is *proof-of-work among registered, metered identities*. Hardened so that no single failure, vendor, buyer, or government-sized tantrum has an off switch.

- **No external hash.** Rented SHA-256, an S19 fleet, or a state **cannot produce a valid block** without a registered identity and element co-signatures at capped rate (§4).
- **Most-work chain selection (changed in v0.4).** Hard window finality is **removed**. A hard no-reorg rule can split the chain permanently after a long network partition. Nodes follow the valid chain with the most accumulated work, as Bitcoin does, so separated networks **heal** on reconnection. Immutability is then economic and grows with depth. §7's one-window settlement delay ensures payouts are only made from deeply buried blocks.
- **No flash majority.** Rewriting or censoring recent history needs a majority of *eligible* systems. Eligibility cannot be bought quickly. The activation queue (§4) caps new eligible systems at +10% of the eligible set per window, behind a filled bond and 56 days of credited work. From 10,000 eligible systems, doubling the eligible set takes ≥8 windows (~224 days) of fully public registrations. At ~$250 all-in per system, a bare majority at 10,000 systems is ~$1.25M in hardware *plus* that time, power, and logistics. The attempt is visible on-chain for months before it can act.
- **No single-vendor blast radius.** ≥3 independent attestation roots, frozen at genesis, anonymous registration (§4). A compromised root pauses only its own new registrations, automatically, on cryptographic fraud proofs. Existing miners, from every vendor, are unaffected.
- **No governance capture of the rules.** There are no admin keys, no upgrade keys, no discretionary slashing, and the commons (§9) cannot alter consensus. Issuance (§5), caps (§5), payout shares (§7), eligibility (§4), and attestation roots are fixed at genesis. Changing them requires a voluntary hard fork that every node operator may simply refuse. The un-upgraded chain keeps running.
- **Cheap to validate forever.** Consensus state (identities, bonds, window accounts) is commitment-based and prunable. The design target is a full node on Pi-class hardware indefinitely. A chain only ordinary people can verify is a chain no one has to trust.
- **No infrastructure dependency.** No central registry, seed service, foundation server, or official miner exists in the protocol's operation. Peer discovery is multi-channel (hardcoded seeds as hints only, plus DNS, Tor, and manual peers). The software is open-source with reproducible builds. At least two independent implementations are a launch requirement (§11).
- **Tor-native networking and launch (v0.4.2, founder's choice).** Nodes can run entirely as Tor onion services. A node never needs a public IP or any clearnet presence. Node-to-node traffic can be Tor-only for operators who want it (clearnet peering remains available; Tor is a right, not a requirement). The launch itself is published over Tor. The genesis block, node software, and this spec are released through a v3 onion service (mirrored by anyone, anywhere. Killing the mirror kills nothing). The founder publishes and operates under Tor from day one, Satoshi-style. No launch website, account, or server on the clearnet is ever part of the chain's operation.
- **RESIDUAL (still named, not hidden).** Absolute attack cost remains small while the network and the coin's value are small. The safety condition is that participation grows ahead of price (~1,000+ eligible systems per $1 of coin price, per the v0.3 simulation). A patient attacker who buys a genuine majority of real systems over many months cannot be stopped by any rule in this document. Only priced, slowed, and exposed. That is the honest limit of the design. It is the same limit Bitcoin has, reached by a different road.

**Majority attack and recovery: the designed response (v0.4.1, founder-approved).** A bought majority is a planned-for event, not an improvisation:
1. **Its power is bounded by validity.** A majority of producers can censor and reorganize recent blocks. It cannot mint coins, move balances, forge identities, or change any rule. Every full node rejects invalid blocks regardless of who produced them. Theft is not on the menu. Disruption is.
2. **Its window is shallow.** §7's settlement delay means large transfers and all payouts only settle from deeply buried blocks. A majority can contest roughly one settlement window of history. After months of public, queue-limited buildup (§4) that gives the network a season of warning.
3. **Recovery fork (the kill shot).** Registrations are on-chain and timestamped, so an attacking cohort is identifiable as a cohort. If a bought majority acts, honest operators fork from the last block before the attack, carrying forward the eligible set as of a pre-attack snapshot and deactivating the attacking registrations. The attacker's hardware, bonds, and coins remain on the abandoned chain. The economic network continues on the fork: users, nodes, honest miners. The attack therefore destroys the value of its own prize, which is the deterrent. A rational buyer prices in that a successful capture gets forked away. (This is the one place the identity layer pays for itself. Anonymous hashpower cannot be excluded as a cohort. Registered systems can.)

**Autonomy: no operator (FOUNDER'S RULE).** After genesis, *nobody runs this chain*. Not the founder, not a foundation, not the commons. There are no admin keys, pause functions, upgrade keys, or discretionary powers anywhere in the protocol. Software updates are voluntary. Any operator may run any version that follows the genesis rules, forever. The founder's role ends at publishing the genesis block and registering system #1 like everyone else. If every original developer disappeared the day after launch, nothing about the chain's operation changes.

## 9. Commons and firmware (carried from v0.2, tightened)

- **2% of each block reward** to a commons fund (one-identity-one-vote among registered systems), spendable only on: open node software, independent firmware implementations, open device designs, repair documentation. **The commons has no power over consensus**. It cannot change issuance, caps, payout shares, eligibility, attestation roots, or any system's balance or bond (v0.4, §8). Attested devices must run **open, auditable firmware**. A closed firmware monoculture would re-centralize template policy upstream of every other rule (the Avalon Nano's closed firmware would not qualify a device for the dividend until an open build exists).
- Longevity: a node system active for a full epoch keeps its bond whole and earns a commons-paid bonus of one bond-equivalent. Paying operators to repair and keep devices alive across the ~4-year epoch the cap already makes them competitive for.

## 10. What "make it work" changed vs v0.2: summary

1. Node **system** (controller + hash units + one identity + one bond) replaces the undefined "node."
2. SHA-256 returns (Bitaxe-class hardware demands it) and S19 exclusion moves from algorithm-physics to **secure-element identity + rate metering**.
3. Trust root for identity = **silicon-vendor certificate chains**, not a project registry. No gatekeeper is created.
4. Issuance: front-loaded halving **and** pure reverse both replaced by the founder's combined **hump + tail** (28.14M over 40 years under the v0.5.1 smoothed schedule, then 1/block forever).
5. Bond: 50 coins upfront → **5 coins, self-funded from first earnings**. Dividend gated on bond completion.
6. Sharing: from a v0.1 sketch to a **phased dividend (50/75/90%)** with qualification thresholds, finder fees, and worked per-system numbers.
7. Security restated honestly as registered-identity PoW + **window finality**, with the residual (value must not outrun participation) named as the design's main live risk.
8. **(v0.3.1)** Dividend qualification changed from an absolute cap÷6 threshold to a **relative threshold (≥25% of the average system's credited work per window)** plus a **dividend fallback to pro-rata**. Simulation showed the absolute threshold disenfranchised older devices by epoch 3 and left 90% of rewards undistributed by epoch 5. Under the patched rules Gini falls 0.215 → 0.043 across epochs 3-5 and a Gamma-class system earns 79% of an Apollo-class system by epoch 5 (`simulation/results.md`).
9. **(v0.4)** Payouts rebuilt as **49% producer / 28% work / 23% sovereign dividend (√work-weighted)** in all epochs (split revised from the v0.4 values by v0.5.2. See entry 12). Pools legal but dividend-ineligible with protocol-enforced contributor payouts and a 5% operator-fee cap (items 6's phased dividend is superseded).
10. **(v0.4)** Security rebuilt: anonymous multi-vendor attestation, activation queue + eligibility delay, compromised-root containment, **window finality removed** in favour of most-work selection + settlement delay (item 7's finality is superseded), and consensus rules frozen against governance at genesis.
11. **(v0.4.1)** §8 gains the **majority-attack-and-recovery clause** (bounded validity, shallow settlement window, snapshot recovery fork) and the **autonomy clause** (no operator, no admin keys, founder exits at launch; founder's miner is system #1 under identical rules, no premine).
12. **(v0.5.2)** Payout split changed from 55/25/20 to **49% producer / 28% work / 23% sovereign dividend** (decided Oct 26, 2026, on the delegation-sweep modelling in `simulation/results-split-49-25-26.md` and `simulation/results-split-49-28-23.md`). The producer remains the largest single slice. Work and dividend together now take the 51% majority, so the majority of every block goes to broad participation rather than to one winner. The 49/28/23 variant was chosen over 49/25/26 because it keeps about half the sovereignty-premium gain while cutting the worst-state Gini penalty to ~40% of 49/25/26's and halving the delegated-income loss (delegated Gamma -3.7% vs -7.5% at epoch 3). The raised work slice also lifts legacy, new, and below-threshold systems' income. Nothing else changed. Schedule, cap, epochs, tail, commons, and fees are untouched.

## 11. Implementation roadmap (for a non-coder director)

1. **This spec** (done; current version v0.5.2).
2. **Simulation**: **DONE Oct 22, 2026** (`simulation/`). The v0.3 model broke §7's original threshold and passed the rest (`simulation/results.md`). The v0.4 model (`simulation/model_v04.py`, `simulation/results-v0.4.md`) validates the payout-split mechanics, delegated-pool pricing, the √-dividend split premium, and the activation queue. The 49/28/23 split itself was validated by the delegation-sweep modelling (`simulation/results-split-49-28-23.md`).
3. **Testnet prototype**: **v0.1 BUILT Oct 22, 2026** (the testnet folder in this repo). Mechanics live end-to-end in a single-process prototype (software-emulated secure elements with rate meter, bond/activation, capped credited work, 49/28/23 payouts with settlement delay, delegated accounting, legacy identities, conservation verified every window), plus a Stratum bridge so a real Bitaxe can hash on the testnet. Persistence **added Oct 22, 2026** (chain state + all keys saved atomically after every window; live mode resumes with a permanent SYSTEM #1 address, verified end-to-end). The v0.5.1 issuance schedule (smoothed hump + tail) and the epoch-rising credited cap are wired into the live chain (testnet epochs compressed to 10 windows). SEND_COINS transfers settle through a mempool while the chain runs. Remaining v0.2 work: P2P networking between separate machines, header validation, anonymous-attestation group signatures (v0.1 uses plain vendor certs). **v0.5 adopted Oct 22, 2026:** watt cap (80W) + 4-year epochs. **v0.5.1** smoothed the hump steps. **v0.5.2** changed the payout split to 49/28/23 (this document).
4. **Hardware revision**: open Bitaxe-class reference design + secure element + integrated node controller (Apollo-style), built by an existing open-hardware maker, not by the founder. ≥3 attestation vendors shipping before mainnet.
5. **Audit + launch decision**: independent security audit. **Launch gates**: ≥2 independent node implementations, ≥3 attestation vendors with no vendor above ~50% of eligible systems, and eligible-system count ahead of the §8 participation-vs-price safety line. Mainnet only if simulation and testnet survive contact with adversaries. A real-money launch without this step is the one version of this project that is not feasible.
6. **Genesis and founder exit**: genesis is pre-announced publicly with the full rules and attestation roots frozen in the genesis block. **No premine and no founder allocation**. Every coin that ever exists enters through §5 issuance and §7 payouts. The founder registers the first system (system #1) under identical bond, queue, and cap rules, then holds no keys, powers, or role the protocol recognizes. Note the hump schedule (§5) makes this honest by construction. The earliest blocks pay the *smallest* rewards on the entire curve, so being first is worth the least. The founder's advantage is symbolic, not economic. **Launch channel (§8):** announcement, genesis block, and software are published via a Tor onion service, with the founder operating over Tor. Clearnet mirrors may exist but are never authoritative or required.
